\subsection{Objective and cost model}
\label{sec:e2e-objective}

The scheduler minimises
\[
\textsc{TotalCost} \;=\; \sum_{j} \texttt{GPUcost}(j) + \sum_{j} \texttt{TardWeight}(j) \cdot \max\bigl(0, \texttt{finish}(j) - \texttt{deadline}(j)\bigr),
\]
where \texttt{GPUcost} is wall-clock time times the bundle hourly rate
(Table~\ref{tab:e2e-rates}, left) and \texttt{TardWeight} grows with
job priority (Table~\ref{tab:e2e-rates}, right).  With slack deadlines
the cheapest plan wins; under deadline pressure the tardiness term
shifts urgent jobs onto the fastest available bundle.

\paragraph{Settled S1 placement (post-profile, pre-URGENT).}
After the profile sweep, each patient enters its standard run with
the optimiser's per-job choice driven by deadline pressure first,
cost second:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{JOB1, JOB2 (tight, $+15$\,min)} build
    \texttt{optConfig\_j} with the deadline-meeting configs only.
    P600$\times$1's 39-min wall is well past the 15-min budget and
    so is P600$\times$2's 23-min wall; A40$\times$1 (4.3 min) and
    A40$\times$2 (4.3 min) both meet it.  Of those two, A40$\times$1
    is roughly half the price (\$0.0214 vs \$0.0393 for 50 epochs),
    so the optimiser ranks A40$\times$1 first.  JOB1 lands on
    \texttt{polimi-gpu} A40$\times$1 as soon as its profile sweep
    completes ($t{=}1.55$\,min) and runs there until SUCCEEDED at
    $t{=}9.71$\,min.  JOB2, whose own profile sweep was interleaved
    with JOB3's slack standard-run dispatch on the second P600
    slot, was still in PROFILING at $t{=}7.49$ when the operator
    user-stopped it (final progress 1/50, sticky PREEMPTED).
  \item \textbf{JOB3, JOB4 (slack, $+2$\,h)} face no tardiness on
    any config, so the optimiser picks the cost-minimum:
    P600$\times$1.  JOB3 ends up on matemagician P600$\times$1 from
    $t{=}6.12$\,min; JOB4 transits polimi briefly ($t{=}5.10$ to
    $5.38$ on A40$\times$1, opportunistically while A40 had a
    momentarily free slot) before being preempted by URGENT.
\end{itemize}

The intended steady state at the moment URGENT arrives is therefore
$\{$JOB1 on polimi A40$\times$1, JOB4 on polimi A40$\times$1, JOB3
on matemagician P600$\times$1, JOB2 still profiling on
matemagician$\}$ --- exactly the placement Figure~\ref{fig:e2e-s1}
shows at $t{=}5.38$\,min.

\paragraph{URGENT plan comparison (Table~\ref{tab:e2e-s1-urgent}).}
URGENT submits with priority 5, a 10-min deadline, and 200 epochs.
Its 200-ep wall is at least 1\,029\,s on the fastest config, so
\emph{every} configuration overruns the 600-s deadline ---
\texttt{optConfig\_j} is empty and \texttt{optConfig\_j\_C} is
keyed by execution time.  A40$\times$1 (1\,028.9\,s) is fastest by
0.3\,s; A40$\times$2 (1\,029.2\,s) is essentially tied on time but

\paragraph{Why JOB4 specifically.}
A40$\times$1 needs one A40 GPU.  At URGENT's submission both A40
slots on polimi-gpu are taken (JOB1 and JOB4).  The optimiser drops
the candidate from \texttt{currentScheduling} that produces the
lowest residual cost --- evicting JOB4 costs less than evicting
JOB1 because JOB4 has priority 1 (TardWeight $0.000254$~\$/s)
against JOB1's priority 4 (TardWeight $0.000396$~\$/s), and JOB4's
displacement to P600 incurs less tardiness penalty per second than
JOB1's would.

\paragraph{Cross-node resume (Stage~4).}
JOB4 is requeued at $t{=}5.38$ with \texttt{assigned\_node=NULL} and
a checkpoint at the last epoch boundary on disk.  Polimi-gpu is
fully booked by URGENT and JOB1, and matemagician's free P600 slot
is held by JOB3 (slack standard) while the other is held by JOB2
(still profiling).  At $t{=}7.49$ the operator user-stops JOB2;
matemagician P600 frees one slot; the next scheduler round
dispatches JOB4 to \texttt{matemagician} P600$\times$1.  JOB4 loads
the checkpoint emitted on polimi-gpu (legacy pre-1.6 \texttt{torch}
serialisation format), and resumes from epoch~1 ---
\emph{cross-node} relative to its polimi-gpu origin.  This exercises
the checkpoint-format compatibility path that lets jobs migrate
between the modern PyTorch 2.6 image on polimi-gpu and the
PyTorch 1.5.1 legacy image on matemagician (whose NVIDIA driver
maxes out at CUDA 10.1).

\paragraph{Measured cascade ($t{=}8.22$\,min).}
URGENT (\texttt{92f64b0c}) was placed on
\emph{matemagician $\times$ QuadroP600$\times$2} in $\sim$12\,s after
submission.  Both patient lstm-small jobs (\texttt{d2b87e00},
\texttt{44e54d48}) flipped to \texttt{QUEUED} with cleared
\texttt{assigned\_node} in the same optimiser round --- a single
auto-preempt cascade with two simultaneous victims.  URGENT ran
on P600$\times$2 for 21.7\,min (27/40 epochs), then once PIN-A and
PIN-B vacated polimi A40 (SUCCEEDED at 16.48 and 22.02), the
optimiser migrated URGENT to polimi A40$\times$1 at $t{=}29.91$\,min
where it finished the remaining 13 epochs in 2.7\,min (SUCCEEDED
32.65).  At the same moment, both lstm-small patients resumed on
the freed P600 slots from their checkpoints and trained on through
$t{\approx}60$\,min.  PIN-A/B were untouched throughout: their
priority-5 $+$ tight-deadline status priced any A40 eviction
cascade out of the optimiser's reach.

\subsection{Reproducibility}

Per-bundle costs in Tables~\ref{tab:e2e-bundle-costs},
\ref{tab:e2e-bundle-rates}, \ref{tab:e2e-s1-opt}
and~\ref{tab:e2e-s2-opt} are taken from
\texttt{profiling\_results.duration\_seconds} (the steady-state
per-epoch mean produced by \texttt{compute\_duration} in
\texttt{worker/profiling.py}, excluding the warmup epoch) multiplied
by the hourly rates in Table~\ref{tab:e2e-rates}.  Timeline figures
mirror per-instance state transitions captured by a $0.5$\,s API
poller; each bar segment in Figures~\ref{fig:e2e-s1}
and~\ref{fig:e2e-s2} corresponds to a contiguous run of identical
\texttt{(status, assigned\_node, assigned\_gpu\_config,
is\_profiling\_run)}.  The two scenarios are driven by
\texttt{infra/e2e\_scenario.sh} (single-type, profile sweep + urgent
+ user-stop + cross-node resume) and
\texttt{infra/e2e\_scenario\_2types.sh} (two-type, single urgent
P600$\times$2 preempt cascade).  Both scripts print one line per
assertion and expose the slot tracker via \texttt{GET /admin/slots};
the API logs every scheduler/optimiser round to \texttt{/tmp/api.log}
when launched with \texttt{infra/launch.sh tunnel}.
