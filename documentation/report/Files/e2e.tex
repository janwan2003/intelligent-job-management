\subsection{Objective and cost model}
\label{sec:e2e-objective}

Every scheduling decision shown below minimises
\[
\textsc{TotalCost} \;=\; \sum_{j} \texttt{GPUcost}(j) + \sum_{j} \texttt{TardWeight}(j) \cdot \max\bigl(0, \texttt{finish}(j) - \texttt{deadline}(j)\bigr).
\]

\texttt{GPUcost} is wall-clock time times the per-hour rate of the
assigned GPU bundle (Table~\ref{tab:e2e-rates}); \texttt{TardWeight}
grows with the job's priority (Table~\ref{tab:e2e-tardweight}).  When
every deadline has slack, the tardiness sum vanishes and the optimiser
picks the \emph{cheapest} placement; when any deadline binds, the
tardiness term dominates and the urgent job is shifted onto the
\emph{fastest} available bundle even if that bundle is more expensive.

\subsection{Two-GPU benefit \textemdash{} where it actually appears}
\label{sec:e2e-multi-gpu}

The heavy CNN type \texttt{cnn\_big} (ten $3{\times}3$ conv blocks
growing $3\to64\to128\to256\to384\to512$ channels on
$128{\times}128{\times}3$ synthetic tensors, batch $32$) was designed
so per-step compute dominates the DataParallel sync overhead on
\emph{both} GPU classes in the cluster.  Table~\ref{tab:e2e-2gpu}
shows the per-epoch wall-clock times measured by the profile sweep,
taken verbatim from the optimiser's input CSV
(\texttt{call\_1778618087.0/SelectJobs\_times.csv}) for the
\texttt{cnn\_big} probe (jobs \texttt{7c2353df}, \texttt{0c23432f}).

\begin{table}[h!]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r}\toprule
Bundle & per-epoch [s] & rel.\ to 1-GPU & per-epoch GPUcost [\$] \\\midrule
\texttt{A40$\times$1}        & 6.294 & --                          & 0.000525 \\
\texttt{A40$\times$2}        & \textbf{5.626} & \textbf{1.12$\times$ \emph{faster}} & 0.000859 \\
\texttt{QuadroP600$\times$1} & 51.064 & --                          & 0.000426 \\
\texttt{QuadroP600$\times$2} & \textbf{30.322} & \textbf{1.68$\times$ \emph{faster}} & 0.000463 \\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Measured per-epoch times from the profile sweep for
\texttt{cnn\_big} (10 conv blocks, $128{\times}128{\times}3$ input,
batch 32).  \textbf{Both} GPU classes now show a 2-GPU wall-clock
speedup: A40$\times$2 is 11\% faster than A40$\times$1, and
P600$\times$2 is \textbf{68\% faster} than P600$\times$1 (1.68$\times$
speedup) --- DataParallel delivers near-linear scaling on P600
because each step's forward+backward pass is large enough to fully

but still big enough that the 2-GPU split clears the NCCL
overhead.}\label{tab:e2e-2gpu}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Does the optimiser ever \emph{pick} 2-GPU?}  At 40 epochs
the four \texttt{cnn\_big} plans cost \$0.0210 (A40$\times$1),
\$0.0344 (A40$\times$2), \$0.0170 (P600$\times$1), and \$0.0185
(P600$\times$2).  For a patient job the optimiser picks
\texttt{P600$\times$1} (cheapest); A40$\times$2 stays the worst
choice because its 11\% wall-clock saving never pays for the 64\%
hourly-rate hike.  But \texttt{P600$\times$2} is only 9\% more
expensive than \texttt{P600$\times$1} and 41\% \emph{faster}
(1213\,s vs 2042\,s wall-clock for 40 epochs), so any deadline that
the P600$\times$1 plan misses by more than 3.4\,s
($0.0015 / 0.000444 = 3.4$\,s at priority~5) flips the optimiser's
ranking to \texttt{P600$\times$2}.  This is the cell that lights up
in Stage~3 of Scenario~2 (Section~\ref{sec:e2e-s2}).

\paragraph{Why the LSTM types never show a 2-GPU win.}
\texttt{lstm-small} on P600 measures 5.47\,s (x1) vs 10.66\,s (x2)
--- 2-GPU is 95\% \emph{slower} because each minibatch's per-step
compute is dominated by the sequential RNN forward and the
DataParallel gradient sync.  \texttt{lstm-big} is closer (21.38\,s
vs 19.41\,s, $\approx 9\%$ faster on x2) but still loses on cost.
The published behaviour of DataParallel on small-batch RNN workloads
matches: only CNN-shaped per-step graphs with enough FLOPs amortise
the sync, which is exactly what \texttt{cnn\_big} delivers.
